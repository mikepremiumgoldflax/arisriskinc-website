About

Physics-based wildfire risk, building by building.

ARIS builds physics-based wildfire models that work building by building: when fire reaches a home and how hard it hits.

ARIS builds physics-based wildfire models that work building by building: when fire reaches a home and how hard it hits. The model simulates fire across fuel, terrain and weather over ten thousand simulated seasons. Exceedance-probability output is built to plug into Oasis, the loss-modelling tools insurers already use. We are early.

We test ourselves in the open. We seal our predictions before the season, publish the proof, and grade them against what happened. Most models are judged on history. We also grade ours forward. Every prediction we publish is sealed in advance and checkable by anyone. In public, the architecture is physics-based, two-stage, sealed and graded.

What works today, and what does not yet

What works today is the wildfire model, scored building by building, including mitigation targeting, planning and rescoring on the same run, plus portfolio and accumulation views. What we are building is the Prairie extension for 2027, delivered with Saskatchewan field partners. What is planned is response intelligence, a concept demonstration in 2027.

Where the work sits

The model was built in California. It is being extended to the Canadian Prairies for 2027. ARIS is based in Saskatchewan. Model data is stored in Canada, on dedicated compute in Canada.

California now allows forward-looking catastrophe models in rate filings.
