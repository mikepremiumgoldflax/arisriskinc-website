California now allows forward-looking models in rate filings

California now allows forward-looking catastrophe models in rate filings. That is a permission. It is not a requirement to buy a catastrophe model, and it is not a requirement to buy ARIS.

How a carrier uses that permission is a filing question. This note is not a compliance opinion.

Where the engine fits

Mitigation on ARIS is a use of the wildfire model, not a second product and not a certificate of compliance. Before the season, the same engine can target where work changes when fire reaches a home and how hard it hits. It can support the plan for that work. After the work is done, it can rescore the building.

What works today is that building-by-building score, with portfolio and accumulation views and exceedance-probability output built to plug into Oasis. We are early. Response intelligence — the view while a fire is underway — is planned as a concept demonstration in 2027.

What stays checkable

The model is physics-based and works building by building. In public, the architecture is physics-based, two-stage, sealed and graded. We seal our predictions before the season, publish the proof, and grade them against what happened. Every prediction we publish is sealed in advance and checkable by anyone.
