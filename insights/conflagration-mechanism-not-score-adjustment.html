Conflagration Is a Mechanism, Not a Score Adjustment

The most expensive wildfire losses of the last two years were not really wildfires in the forest sense. They were urban conflagrations, fire moving house to house once it reached the built environment. The modeling industry has finally conceded the point, and the shape of the concession tells you a great deal about how legacy models were built and where they break.

A correction that indicts the base model

Cotality's 2026 Wildfire Risk Report estimates that more than 2.5 million properties across the 10 most exposed western states face moderate or greater wildfire risk, representing nearly $1.4 trillion in reconstruction cost value. The methodological headline, though, is not the exposure total. It is that layering conflagration potential onto a traditional wildfire score can add up to 40 points to a property's rating, pushing meaningful hazard into neighborhoods legacy maps previously classified as low risk.

Read that as an admission. If a single mechanism moves a risk score by 40 points, the base model was effectively blind to the mechanism that produced the most severe recent losses. Cotality is candid that traditional models, which emphasize terrain and vegetation, can understate this neighborhood-level hazard. The fix on offer is an additive markup on top of the same coarse surface. That is not the same thing as modeling the mechanism.

The mechanism, not the markup

What actually burned Los Angeles in January 2025 is well documented. Moody's reconnaissance attributes the severity of urban losses to connective fuels, older construction such as single-pane windows, ember pathways, and structure-to-structure spread under high winds, with early IBHS findings describing a textbook coupling of wind vectors and street orientation that turned neighborhoods into fuel rather than breaks. The Palisades Fire burned roughly 23,448 acres and the Eaton Fire about 14,021 acres, with over 18,000 structures destroyed or damaged. The destruction was urban, not wildland.

Carriers that account for these factors upfront can make sure homes are properly insured for the catastrophe they actually face, not just the forest fire, but the fire next door.

The reason a markup cannot substitute for the mechanism is that structure-to-structure ignition is irreducibly a parcel-level physics problem. Whether your house catches depends on the house thirty feet away, on roof and vent hardening, on building materials, on structure density, and on where embers land under a given wind field. A county or ZIP aggregate cannot represent any of that, and an adjustment bolted onto such an aggregate inherits its blindness. You are smearing a precise, local phenomenon across a geography that averages away the very differences that decide the loss.

The encouraging news is that the science is tractable at the right resolution. USC Viterbi researchers have built a physics-based system that forecasts a fire's path, intensity and growth by combining satellite data with simulation, and their recent work adds terrain effects such as slope and elevation, because fire tends to spread faster upslope, trained on real wildfires rather than generic scenarios. Ignition, fuel, terrain and wind are modelable drivers. They are not well captured by a statistical overlay.

Why resolution is now a pricing question

This has stopped being academic. The same report finds that homes in the top decile of mitigation carry expected losses roughly 78% below the statewide average, while the bottom decile runs more than ten times that average, about $47 in expected loss for every $1 on the best-prepared homes. A model that cannot see the parcel cannot see that spread, and therefore cannot price it. Meanwhile the backstop is straining: the California FAIR Plan will raise rates by an average of 29.1% starting in October, with nearly 670,000 homes insured and total exposure of $724 billion. Mispriced tails flow straight into the insurer of last resort.

The question for any carrier or ILS desk is whether its view of wildfire models the fire next door or merely annotates a map that cannot see it. Conflagration is a mechanism. It deserves to be modeled as one.
