The house that burned next to the house that didn't

After a wildfire, one home can be lost and the next one left standing. The fire followed fuel, slope, wind and embers. The loss happened at the structure.

Two homes on the same street can face different fuel, a different slope, and a different path for embers. One may have cleared space around it and a less combustible roof. The other may back onto brush that channels wind. A single number for the whole district describes the district. It does not describe either structure.

The model works building by building because the loss does.

What the model estimates

ARIS simulates fire across fuel, terrain and weather over ten thousand seasons. For each home it estimates when fire reaches it and how hard it hits. What works today is that building-by-building score. The same scores roll up to portfolio and accumulation views, and to exceedance-probability output built to plug into Oasis. We are early.

The catalogue is the same engine used for mitigation. Before the season it can target where work changes that result. After the work is done, the building can be rescored.

Sealed at that resolution

We seal our predictions before the season, publish the proof, and grade them against what happened. The 2026 California forecast was sealed building by building, before the season. The grading method was sealed with it. Every prediction we publish is sealed in advance and checkable by anyone.
