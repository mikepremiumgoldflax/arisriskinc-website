The house that burned next to the house that didn't

After a wildfire, one home can be lost and the next one left standing. The fire followed fuel, slope, wind and embers. The loss happened at the structure.

Two homes on the same street can face different fuel, a different slope, and a different path for embers. One may have cleared space around it and a less combustible roof. The other may back onto brush that channels wind. A single number for the whole district describes the district. It does not describe either structure.

The model is parcel-resolved because the loss is.

What the model estimates

ARIS simulates fire across fuel, terrain and weather over ten thousand seasons. For each structure it estimates the probability that fire reaches it, and the probability that it is lost. Those probabilities are the pricing-grade parcel scores. The same scores roll up to portfolio and accumulation views, and to exceedance-probability output in open insurer standards.

The catalogue is the same engine used for mitigation. Before the season it can target where work changes that probability. After the work is done, the parcel can be rescored.

Sealed at that resolution

The 2026 California forecast was sealed at the parcel, before the season. The grading method was sealed with it. The grade is published whatever it says. Results are 100% reproducible bit-for-bit.
